06 - KNOWN CHALLENGES
======================

These are the real technical difficulties for this specific scenario.
Understanding them upfront prevents building the wrong solutions.


CHALLENGE 1: BALL DETECTION AND OCCLUSION
-------------------------------------------

The basketball is a small, fast-moving object.

In a 10-minute video at 30fps, the ball is visible and trackable in
approximately 60-70% of frames. The rest of the time it is:

  - Held in a player's hands and partially hidden
  - Mid-flight and motion-blurred
  - Behind another player's body
  - Out of frame during a long pass

Impact: If the system loses the ball 10 frames before a shot, it cannot
reliably detect the shot.

Solution: ByteTrack's Kalman filter predicts the ball's position when
it is not detected. When the ball reappears, the prediction is corrected.
This is built into ByteTrack. We do not write this ourselves.

Reality check: The system will still miss some shots where the ball is
occluded for an extended period before release.


CHALLENGE 2: MADE BASKET DETECTION (HARDEST PROBLEM)
------------------------------------------------------

Detecting a shot attempt is easier than detecting it went in.
A confirmed made basket requires observing:

  1. Ball enters hoop area from above
  2. Ball travels downward through the cylinder
  3. Ball exits below the rim

This is camera-angle dependent:

  - Courtside angle (side view): The ball passing through the hoop is
    visible. This is the best case.

  - Far-back angle: The ball approaches the rim and disappears.
    The system must infer the make from context:
      → Ball reappears below the rim
      → Possession changes to the other team
      → No rebound motion visible

  - Overhead angle: Ball passing through is clearly visible but this
    camera position is rare for amateur recordings.

Practical limitation: The MVP will miss some made baskets and may
occasionally count a near-miss (ball hits rim and bounces away) as a make.
This is unavoidable at this stage.

That is why confidence scores are shown per event. A "made basket (0.61)"
tells the user to verify that one.


CHALLENGE 3: SHOT ATTRIBUTION — WHO SHOT IT?
---------------------------------------------

The system must correctly identify which player released the ball.

Difficulty: A catch-and-shoot in one motion is hard to distinguish from
a pass. The ball may be mid-flight between a passer and a shooter just
as the shooter is catching it.

Rule used:
  The player with possession in the frames immediately before the ball's
  velocity becomes directed at the hoop is the shooter.

  Specifically:
    → Find the frame where ball separates from all player bounding boxes
    → Look back 10-15 frames
    → The player whose box most recently overlapped with the ball is the shooter
    → That player's team gets the point

This works well for standard shot sequences. It can fail on quick catch-
and-shoot plays where the catch and release happen within 3-5 frames.


CHALLENGE 4: 2-POINT VS 1-POINT (THE ARC PROBLEM)
----------------------------------------------------

In FIBA 3x3 basketball:
  - 1 point for shots inside the arc
  - 2 points for shots behind the arc

To determine this, the system needs to know:
  1. Where the 3-point arc is in the video frame
  2. Where the shooter's feet were at the moment of release

Court line detection methods:
  - Hough Circle Transform (OpenCV) to detect the arc as a circle
  - Homography: map a known court template onto the video perspective

MVP decision: Skip arc detection entirely for v1.
Award a flat 1 point per made basket regardless of position.
Label this clearly in the UI: "Points (1pt per basket — arc detection in v2)"

This removes significant complexity from v1 and lets us ship something
working. Arc detection is a well-defined problem for v2.


CHALLENGE 5: TRACKING IDENTITY SWAPS
--------------------------------------

When two players cross paths or collide, ByteTrack can occasionally
swap their track IDs — what was Track 3 becomes Track 5 and vice versa.

Impact for our scenario: An identity swap means a basket could be
credited to the wrong team if the swap occurs near a shot event.

Mitigation:
  - Jersey color classification is re-run periodically (every 60 seconds)
    to catch and correct any track that drifted to the wrong team
  - Because teams wear visually distinct colors, a team-level error
    (wrong team gets the point) is much less likely than a player-level
    error (wrong player within the same team)

For v1, team-level accuracy is what matters. Individual player
attribution within a team is not tracked.


CHALLENGE 6: HOOP POSITION STABILITY
--------------------------------------

The hoop position is needed as a reference point for shot detection
and made basket detection.

For most amateur 3v3 recordings, the camera is relatively stable.
The hoop stays in approximately the same position throughout the video.

MVP approach:
  - Detect hoop position in the first 10-20 frames
  - Use Hough Circle Transform or a manual bounding box
  - Treat that position as fixed for the entire video
  - Add a small tolerance zone around it for detection

If the camera pans significantly, the hoop detection will drift.
This is an acceptable limitation for v1.


SUMMARY TABLE
--------------

Challenge                  Severity   v1 Solution
--------------------------  ---------  ------------------------------------------
Ball occlusion             Medium     ByteTrack Kalman filter (built in)
Made basket detection      High       Confidence scoring + user flagging
Shot attribution           Low-Med    Possession tracking, 10-frame lookback
Arc / scoring zone         Low        Skip — flat 1pt per basket for v1
Track identity swaps       Low        Periodic color re-classification
Hoop position drift        Low        Fixed hoop position from early frames
