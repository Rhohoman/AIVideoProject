5. Core AI pipeline for your MVP

Now let’s go deeper into the actual analysis process.

Step 1: Video ingestion

The user uploads a basketball video.

System stores:

original video file
metadata such as:
duration
resolution
fps
FPS

FPS means frames per second.

A video might have:

24 fps
30 fps
60 fps

If you process every frame, it can be expensive.
For MVP, you may only process:

every frame if short video
or every 2nd/3rd frame for efficiency
Step 2: Frame extraction

The worker breaks the video into image frames.

Why?
Because computer vision models often analyze video frame by frame.

For example:

second 1 might have 30 frames
second 2 might have 30 frames
and so on

Now your system has a sequence of images representing the game.

Step 3: Court, player, ball, and hoop detection

This is where computer vision begins.

The model tries to detect objects in each frame:

player bodies
basketball
hoop
possibly court lines
Detection

Detection means:
“Find an object and draw a box around it.”

Output might look like:

Player at box (x1, y1, x2, y2)
Ball at box (x1, y1, x2, y2)
Hoop at box (x1, y1, x2, y2)

This is often called object detection.

Step 4: Player identification

Now the system knows there are players in the frame, but not who they are.

To identify a specific player, you can use:

jersey number
jersey color
team color
player appearance features
motion continuity across nearby frames

This is a combination of several ideas.

OCR

OCR means Optical Character Recognition.

It is used to read text from images.

In your app, OCR would help read:

jersey number like 23, 30, 11

But OCR on sports video is hard because:

players move fast
numbers can be blurry
angles change
players overlap

So jersey number alone is not enough.

Re-identification

Re-identification means:
“Is this the same person I saw earlier?”

Even if player #30 turns around or moves across the court, the system tries to keep the same identity.

This is often shortened to ReID.

For your use case, player ID might be based on:

jersey number
jersey color
body appearance embedding
position continuity over time

That is much stronger than using just OCR.

Step 5: Multi-object tracking

This is one of the most important concepts.

If the model detects 10 players in frame 1 and 10 players in frame 2, how do you know which one is the same player across time?

That is the job of tracking.

Tracking

Tracking means assigning a stable ID over time.

Example:

frame 1: detected player A
frame 2: same person is still player A
frame 3: still player A

So internally you might have:

Track 101 = blue jersey #30
Track 102 = white jersey #7

This matters because actions happen over time, not just in one frame.

Without tracking, your app would just see random detections.
With tracking, your app can say:

player #30 ran to the wing
then received the ball
then dribbled
then shot

That is how you move from image detection to sports understanding.

Step 6: Ball possession estimation

To create basketball stats, you need to know who has the ball.

This is tricky.

The system can estimate possession by:

distance between ball and player hands/body
movement synchronization
whether ball remains close to the player over several frames

This is not always perfect, but it is crucial.

Example:

ball is closest to tracked player for 20 consecutive frames
likely that player is dribbling or carrying possession
Step 7: Action recognition

Once players and ball are tracked, the system tries to determine actions.

Possible actions:

dribble
pass
shot attempt
made shot
rebound
steal
block

There are two broad ways to do this.

Option 1: Rule-based event logic

Use object positions and motion to infer actions.

Example:

player had possession
ball leaves player trajectory toward hoop
ball reaches hoop area
ball passes downward through rim area
count made shot

This is simpler and more explainable.

Option 2: Action recognition model

Use a trained video model that looks at short clips and predicts an action label.

Example:

clip from 12.0s to 13.5s = “jump shot”

This can be more powerful, but harder to build well.

For MVP, the best design is usually:

use tracking + rules
optionally add action classification later

That gives you a stronger foundation.
